# The Full Boring Version v13

Use only when the user explicitly asks for a full report, complete analysis, everything, or the **full boring version**.

Start with:

> # Insights You Can Use Now

Do not start with methodology or a dataset inventory. Put the compact coverage note immediately below the heading only when there is a parsing/coverage warning; otherwise place it in Dataset Coverage.

Scale every section to the evidence. Omit unsupported sections and combine overlapping ones. A full report means comprehensive use of the corpus, not mandatory padding.

## 1. Insights You Can Use Now

Give three to five distinct insights maximum. Cover different branches, not variations of one idea. Each insight needs evidence IDs, status, strength, and the strategic consequence.

End with one plain-language synthesis of what matters most.

## 2. Branch Map

Show the evidence-backed hub:

| Branch | Strongest finding | Status | Strength | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Include only supported branches. Mark important weak signals as `watch`, not as recommendations. This table prevents the report from collapsing into one execution path.

## 3. Value, Habit, and Product Truth

Explain what detailed positive reviews say the product reliably does for people, what creates repeat use, and what value language deserves protection. Separate durable value from generic praise.

## 4. Friction, Reliability, and Recovery

Disambiguate problems with different causes or remedies. Show failure sequences, rating/version/date concentration, and whether support recovery changes the language or severity.

## 5. Activation and Commitment Moments

Cover onboarding, first value, registration, permissions, payment, cancellation, renewal, or repeated use only when supported. Use Commitment Flow Architecture (CFA) sparingly and only with Relevance Gap, Desire Gap, Trust Gap, or Ability Gate. Label CFA diagnoses INFERENCE.

## 6. Monetization and Value Exchange

Include only when reviews mention payment, pricing, trials, subscriptions, billing, cancellation, premium access, ads, or perceived value.

Separate:

- price objection;
- timing or sequencing objection;
- trust/fairness perception;
- billing or cancellation friction;
- insufficient premium value;
- willingness-to-pay language.

Treat the current model as a deliberate tradeoff. Review evidence can justify tests, not revenue forecasts.

## 7. Customer Language and Positioning

Extract five to twelve useful phrases at most. Preserve original text and Review IDs. Include English meaning outside the quote for non-English phrases.

Separate:

- value language worth reusing;
- expectation language revealing the promise users thought they bought;
- complaint language useful for diagnosis but unsafe or unsuitable for marketing.

Do not promote an unverified medical, legal, financial, privacy, safety, or guaranteed-outcome claim simply because a reviewer wrote it.

## 8. ASO and Creative Direction

Give strategic territories, not finished assets, unless execution was explicitly requested. Connect each territory to the evidence, the audience tension/value, a possible message direction, and claims to avoid.

## 9. Competitor, Segment, and Market Context

Include alternatives, switching triggers, underserved contexts, rating bands, versions, dates, languages, countries, or storefronts when supported. Analyze each source separately before cross-source conclusions.

Do not convert contextual reviewer descriptions into demographic market facts.

## 10. What Could Be Tested

Provide a compact prioritized set of review-backed hypotheses:

| Hypothesis or direction | Branch | Evidence | Expected learning | Status/strength |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank by evidence strength, reversibility, and learning value when those factors are knowable. Do not pretend reviews establish implementation cost, commercial impact, or roadmap priority.

## 11. What Needs Analytics

List the consequential questions reviews cannot answer, such as:

- whether a complaint pattern affects conversion, retention, churn, support volume, or revenue;
- how frequent the issue is in the full user population;
- whether a version, market, or cohort difference persists in behavioral data;
- whether a proposed change improves an outcome.

Name the minimum metric, event, cohort split, experiment, or research needed to validate each question when useful.

## 12. Dataset Coverage and Limits

State compactly:

- supplied, parsed, excluded, and analyzed counts;
- denominator used for all percentages;
- platform, storefront/country, date range, ratings, versions, languages, and source when available;
- whether the sample is latest, selected, visible, filtered, incomplete, or unknown;
- material deduplication, parsing, language, or long-corpus limitations.

Do not call the export representative or complete without evidence.

## 13. Evidence Appendix

Include only the most decision-relevant references by default:

| Review ID | Rating/date/version/source | Theme | Short verbatim evidence | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Provide a full evidence ledger only when explicitly requested or when the user needs an audit artifact.

## Ending

A full report may end cleanly. Add at most one next door only when a distinct, evidence-backed branch deserves separate work. Do not automatically push the user toward copywriting, creative production, or another report.
