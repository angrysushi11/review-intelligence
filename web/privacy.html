What the website processes

When you ask for reviews, Review Intel processes the App Store or Google Play listing you gave it, the public review text and the public details that come with it: rating, date, app version, reviewer name and developer reply. It uses them to return the reviews you asked for.

The website never asks for App Store Connect or Play Console credentials. Don’t send credentials, private exports or anything confidential.

What the MCP server processes

The public MCP endpoint is read-only and needs no account or OAuth login. It processes the app, the market, the sort order, the number of reviews requested and, on Google Play, the continuation cursor for the next batch.

Each response holds up to 500 reviews. Google Play can return a cursor for more; Apple’s public feed has no equivalent.

Storage and infrastructure

The app’s code has no review database or persistence layer: each request is processed to produce its response. That doesn’t mean no metadata exists anywhere. Vercel, network providers and Apple’s or Google’s services may keep ordinary request or operational logs under their own policies.

Public review text and reviewer names can still be personal data, even when anyone can read them. Handle downloaded files under the rules that apply to your use.

Website analytics

The pages use Google Analytics. Review Intel sends a cleaned page location and referrer, without the app link, query parameters or fragment, and cuts a referrer down to its origin.

If a link carries supported UTM fields, only a validated campaign ID and the source, medium, campaign, content or term labels are sent, never the full query string. Other sources are reduced to an internal path, an external hostname or a short label.

Analytics records coarse product events, such as starting or finishing a review collection, opening an analysis destination or choosing a setup link. When a visit comes from a recognized AI assistant, it may also get a coarse label such as ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Gemini or Copilot. None of this is used for advertising or sold.

AI providers and evidence limits

The web tool can prepare reviews for a destination you choose, such as Claude or ChatGPT. Those providers process the data under their own terms and privacy policies. The MCP endpoint returns reviews to the client that called it, and that client decides how it stores or uses them.

Reviews show patterns in the sample you collected. On their own, they don’t prove revenue, retention, conversion, causality or what every user thinks.

Chrome extension

The optional Chrome extension does less. After you click it, it reads the current app-listing URL and opens Review Intel with that link in the form. Its own policy lists the exact permissions and handling.

Your choices

You can use the website without an account. If you don’t want an app link and its reviews processed through these services, don’t start a collection or connect the MCP server. Your browser’s privacy controls or a content blocker can stop the analytics.
